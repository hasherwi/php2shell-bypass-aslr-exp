# Presentation readiness checklist

## Current status

This is a documentation and presentation review, not a runtime validation.
No exploit execution or independent reproduction was performed for this update.
The inherited README results, timings, sample output, and linked logs describe
upstream-reported historical work. They do not establish that this fork works
in a current environment or that a live demonstration is ready.

This checklist does not authorize execution or provide setup, exploitation,
or troubleshooting instructions. Completing a documentation checklist is not
a substitute for separate safety approval and technical validation.

## Scope and safety review

- [ ] Identify the owner of any proposed lab and record explicit authorization
  for the intended scope.
- [ ] Confirm that any proposed lab is disposable and isolated from production,
  shared services, other users, and sensitive data.
- [ ] Explain that worker exhaustion, memory exhaustion, process crashes, and
  service interruptions are possible. Automatic restarts do not guarantee
  availability or recovery.
- [ ] Identify a responsible person and a stop decision if isolation,
  authorization, or service impact is uncertain.
- [ ] Use a conceptual walkthrough or clearly labeled historical material if
  live readiness has not been separately established.

## Evidence and claims

- [ ] Label each result as an upstream report, a new observation, or an
  unverified claim.
- [ ] Keep the original date, source link, and revision with any historical
  excerpt. Do not present archived sample output as a fresh result.
- [ ] Keep reported success counts and timings tied to their stated context;
  do not generalize them to other systems or claim guaranteed success.
- [ ] Record failed, incomplete, and not-run work separately. Do not convert a
  missing result into a pass.
- [ ] Review displayed excerpts for credentials, personal data, internal
  addresses, or other material not approved for the presentation audience.

## Presentation outline

1. State the educational purpose, audience, and authorized scope.
2. Explain the security issue at a conceptual level and its possible impact.
3. Present source attribution and the limits of the historical evidence.
4. Explain the lab-only safety boundary and the risk of service interruption.
5. Close with defensive lessons, unanswered questions, and the current
   validation status.

Before presenting:

- [ ] Check that links and language navigation work.
- [ ] Keep the English, Chinese, and Japanese warnings consistent.
- [ ] Clearly identify screenshots or excerpts as historical where applicable.
- [ ] Prepare a non-live explanation so a presentation does not depend on
  unverified execution.
- [ ] State remaining uncertainties rather than implying demo readiness.
